# Teaching note: the nine-slice pie

A pie chart asks the eye to compare angles, which it does badly — and with nine slices, five of them become slivers whose labels pile on top of each other. The chart ends up communicating exactly one thing ("Grants is biggest") while pretending to communicate nine. The fix is sorted horizontal bars: length against a shared baseline is the comparison humans get right, and grouping the crumbs into "Other" keeps the chart scannable. Practical rule: more than five or six categories, skip the pie; more than ten, start grouping.
