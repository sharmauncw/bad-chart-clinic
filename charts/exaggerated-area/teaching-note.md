# Teaching note: the exaggerated area

An area chart doesn't just show a line — it fills the space beneath it, so the reader's brain registers the *size of the colored region* as the size of the phenomenon. Starting the axis at 10,000 turns a 3.9% drift into a mountain range; the ink shouts while the data murmurs. This is the same sin as the truncated bar axis, wearing fancier clothes. The fix is identical in spirit: anchor the fill at zero, let the true shape appear, and annotate the actual percent change. If a 3.9% increase is the story, say "3.9%" — don't draw it like 390%.
